It's November 2026, and the answer to this question is an overriding yes. With technology as it is today, the only thing you have to pay for at the start of your project is your domain name. That's a yearly fee, often as low as €10 for the whole year. After that, there are several places where your new website can be hosted completely free. How you get to the point of having a fully built website, and what you need that website to do, is the crux of it. Those two things decide what further payments, if any, you need to make.

I want to start with a quick look at what's out there at the moment. The big players, Squarespace, GoDaddy, WordPress, Wix and the rest, all work on a subscription model. Many projects and businesses only need a web presence: an informational site, maybe with a contact form so people can get in touch. The cost of actually hosting a site like that is very small, especially for these big companies who run their own servers and infrastructure. So what are you actually paying for?

The simple answer is that they give you the tools to create the website. On the face of it, that sounds like a very good deal. Depending on the provider, there's a whole range of tools for creating a design within the limits of their templates. But what happens next is what keeps you inside their subscription model for a long time. You can't shop around for where your site is hosted, because the catch is that you have to host with them. You could have made a beautiful Squarespace site, but if you fall out with them, or hit the usual hurdle of wanting to change something their tool doesn't offer, that site would need building again from scratch somewhere else. That's time and money.

And the price rarely goes down. In France this year Wix renamed its plans and the cheapest one with your own domain went from around €13 a month to €16.80. Some hosts advertise a couple of euros a month, but only if you pay for four years up front, and the price roughly quadruples when it renews. Over three or four years, a "cheap" monthly plan can easily cost more than having a site built.

WordPress sits at an interesting crossroads. There are quite a few DIY options within it, but some of them get technical enough that you end up paying a designer to use the tool for you. I'm not here to argue for or against WordPress, or to put you through another one of my rants, so I'd encourage you to read my [previous post on WordPress](https://www.awattsdev.eu/en/blog/should-i-get-a-wordpress-website).

The other option is to pay someone to build the site for you. In that case they are the tool. And going back to what I said earlier, it depends on what you need the site to do. Those small to medium sites don't need much maintenance, or at least not regular maintenance. They're perfect candidates for free hosting, and Cloudflare and Netlify are two of the best options out there. But I'm going to go a little further here, it's possible to build something completely free to host and go a bit beyond the usual informational site model. Say you sold a service and wanted to take online payments, and those products stayed fairly static. There's no reason that couldn't be hosted free either. You'd still pay the payment provider a small fee on each sale, but that's a cost of selling, not a subscription. Maybe you were an artist and wanted to upload new images of your work to your site, this is possible to do without a monthly subscription too, within the free allowance, which is generous for a small portfolio. These are solid options, but now I'm starting to get into 'it depends' territory.

So when is a monthly fee the right answer? When your website stops being a shop window and starts doing real work. An online shop with stock and products that change regularly, a booking system, customer accounts with logins, a members area, anything that keeps information about your customers. Sites like that run on a database and a server that are switched on all day, every day, and that costs real money to run. They also need looking after. The software needs regular security updates, the backups need checking, and when a payment provider changes its rules, someone has to make sure the site keeps up.

Then there's the data. As soon as your site stores your customers' names, addresses, orders or messages, you're responsible for that information under GDPR (RGPD here in France). It has to be kept safe, only kept as long as you need it, and if something goes wrong you have obligations to report it. That isn't something to leave on a free plan and forget about. For a site like this, paying monthly for hosting and for someone to keep an eye on it isn't a trap, it's the sensible thing to do. I offer that myself for exactly these projects.

One honest note about "free". Your domain still renews every year, and if you want an email address on that domain, like contact@yourbusiness.fr, that usually comes with its own small yearly cost from the domain registrar or an email provider. Neither of those is a monthly subscription to a website builder, and neither ties your site to anyone. But it's better you hear it from me now than find it on a bill later.

Whichever way you go, there's one question worth asking before you pay anyone anything: if I stop paying, what do I keep? Is the domain registered in my name? Do I have the code, or at least my content, in a form I can take somewhere else? With a site you've had built and hosted in your own accounts, the answer is everything. With a builder subscription, the answer is usually nothing, and you start again.

So yes, you can have a website without a monthly subscription, and for a lot of small businesses it's the better choice. If your site needs to manage bookings, customer accounts or store your customers' data, a monthly fee makes sense, as long as you know what it's paying for and you still own what you've paid to build.
